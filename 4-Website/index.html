01

The short version

The Act says

The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 adds two new kinds of financial product to the Corporations Act: digital asset platforms (DAPs) and tokenised custody platforms (TCPs). S1 · Sch 1 item 2, s 764A(1)(la)–(lb)

ASIC says
  • What changes: businesses that run these platforms will need an Australian financial services (AFS) licence, unless an exemption applies. They will also have to give customers disclosure documents and platform rules, and meet ASIC standards on holding assets, trading and settlement, and capital. S3 · 26-245MR S6 · RG 000.7–000.8
  • When: Royal Assent was 8 April 2026. The Act starts on 8 April 2027, which is also when licence applications open. Platforms already operating must apply by 7 October 2027 and can keep operating while ASIC assesses them. S1 · s 2 S5 · p 5, para 45 Why not 9 April?
The Act says

Who is not caught: small platforms that meet strict low-value conditions; people who only tell others about a platform, or arrange for them to use it, as a minor part of their business; and qualifying public digital token infrastructure, along with the people who validate it. S1 · s 911A(2)(ja)–(jb), s 9E, s 766A(3)(b)

Our analysis

For issuers using Tokeniser: the Act regulates platforms, not the act of issuing a token. A fund or company that issues its units or shares on-chain still has to follow the existing rules for that product. The new rules matter if anyone holds tokens or keys for investors, or if an issuer creates one token for each asset it holds in custody. The biggest open question is whether Redbelly meets the Act's test for "public digital token infrastructure". S6 · RG 000.35, 000.46–000.48 S12 · Technology

02

What the Act is, and why it was introduced

ASIC says

The Act carries out the Government's commitment to modernise Australia's rules for digital assets. It fills regulatory gaps that left the industry unsure where it stood. Its aims are to foster innovation, strengthen competition and growth, and strengthen Australia's position in the global digital asset ecosystem. It amends both the Corporations Act and the ASIC Act. S5 · paras 2–5

ASIC's approach follows the principle of "same activity, same risk, same regulatory outcome". ASIC Commissioner Alan Kirkland said that bringing these platforms into the financial services framework "means consumers can have greater confidence when trading digital assets". S3 · 26-245MR

ASIC also says the regime cannot remove all risk. Many digital assets are high-risk speculative investments and will stay that way, and consumers could lose the entire value of their investment. S5 · para 14

The reforms bring Australia closer to overseas regimes, including the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), Singapore and Hong Kong, which followed IOSCO's 2023 recommendations. S5 · paras 8–9

ASIC says

It builds on existing law. ASIC's view is that financial services law is broad and technology-neutral and already applies to many digital assets. In October 2025 ASIC updated its Information Sheet 225 (INFO 225), which lists tokenised securities among the digital assets it treats as financial products. It also gave a sector-wide "no-action" position to firms moving to licensing. That deadline was first 30 June 2026 and was later extended to 30 September 2026. S9 · 25-250MR S8 · 25 June 2026 S3 · Background

Our analysis

Before this Act, whether a crypto exchange or custodian needed a licence depended mostly on whether the tokens it handled were themselves financial products. Under the Act, the platform itself is the financial product, separate from the tokens on it. So the platform needs a licence whatever kind of tokens it holds. ASIC puts it this way: the platform is a financial product, while the assets on it "may or may not be". S6 · RG 000.20 S5 · para 53

The key ideas in plain English

TermWhat it meansSource
Digital tokenAn electronic record that someone can "factually control": they can transfer it, stop others transferring it, and show that they can do both. A bank's account ledger is not a digital token, because the bank changes the balance rather than moving the record.S1 · s 761GB(1)–(2), Note 3
PossessionYou "possess" a token if you can factually control it, and more than one person can. But you do not share possession if you need someone else's express cooperation and they can act alone.S1 · s 86(2)–(3), s 761GB(3)
Digital asset platform (DAP)An arrangement where an operator possesses digital tokens for or on behalf of clients, for example as trustee or on the client's instructions. ASIC says an operator may simply hold tokens, or may also offer buying, selling, swapping, staking or lending.S1 · s 761GC S6 · RG 000.36–000.39
Tokenised custody platform (TCP)An operator holds an asset other than money, creates a single token for each asset, and whoever holds the token can redeem the asset or direct its delivery. ASIC's example is gold held in custody. If something meets both definitions, it is a TCP, not a DAP.S1 · s 761GD, s 761GC(4) S7 · p 1
Public digital token infrastructure (PDTI)Software or hardware used for transmitting, processing or recording tokens. In the Act's words, its protocols must be "a set of open source and publicly accessible standards" that work "in a non-discretionary manner". They must also let any person contribute (for example by validating), and must not rely on any one participant whose role is critical. Infrastructure can also qualify if regulations prescribe it. PDTI is not a managed investment scheme or a financial product, and validating it is not a financial service.S1 · s 9E(2)(a)–(c), (3); s 9 "managed investment scheme" (mf); s 765A(1)(xa); s 766A(3)(b)
Wrapped tokenA token you can redeem for another asset. The redemption right is ignored when deciding whether the wrapped token is a financial product. This does not apply if holders get rights that differ from holding the asset directly.S1 · s 765E
Custodial stakingA platform stakes clients' tokens with their express authority and passes on the rewards after fees. If the platform's issuer holds the right AFS licence, the staking part is not treated as a separate managed investment scheme.S1 · s 9F; s 9 "managed investment scheme" (me)

04

Who needs a licence, and who does not

Running a platform

The Act says
  • DAPs and TCPs become financial products, unless the platform is a managed investment scheme. S1 · s 764A(1)(la)–(lb)
  • Platform services must be provided by, or on behalf of, a "constitutionally-covered corporation", a term the Act defines by reference to the Constitution. Breaching this is a civil penalty matter. S1 · s 766M; s 9
  • A licensed operator must comply with ASIC's asset-holding standards and transactional and settlement standards, have platform rules with set content, and obey any ministerial ban on particular products. S1 · ss 912BB–912BD, 912BG
ASIC says
  • If you operate a DAP or TCP, you need an AFS licence to issue it, unless an exemption applies. S6 · RG 000.8, RG 000.78
  • Draft: ASIC proposes separate "issue" authorisations for five business models: custody-only DAP, DAP acting as agent, DAP acting as principal, DAP as trading venue provider, and TCP operator. S6 · Table 4, RG 000.90
  • Draft: you cannot issue a DAP or TCP as another licensee's corporate authorised representative. The issuer must hold the authorisation itself, apart from a limited related-party exception expected in regulations. S6 · RG 000.82–000.85
  • If a facility is a trading venue for tokens that are financial products, the financial market or clearing and settlement licensing regime can apply instead. S6 · RG 000.60–000.62

People around the platform

The Act says

The Act creates a new kind of "dealing": a client of a platform who uses it on behalf of their own customers is dealing in it. Using a platform for your own internal purposes is not dealing. S1 · s 766C(1)(ca) and note

ASIC says

Draft: you may also need a licence if you advise on a DAP or TCP, arrange for someone else to use one or be issued one, or use one on behalf of others. S6 · RG 000.67, Table 2, RG 000.80

Exemptions

The Act says
  • Low-value DAPs. The issuer does not need a licence only if all four conditions are met:
    1. no financial products are held on any DAP issued by its closely-related group;
    2. the total value of transactions through those DAPs in the last 12 months is $10 million or less;
    3. each time a token is added for a client, that client's total entry value across those DAPs is $5,000 or less;
    4. it has lodged a notice with ASIC saying it relies on the exemption.
    Regulations can raise these amounts. S1 · s 911A(2)(ja), (4B), (6)
  • Limited advice and arranging. You only tell someone a DAP or TCP exists and/or arrange for them to use it, in the ordinary course of your business, and this is not a significant part of your business. S1 · s 911A(2)(jb)
  • Validators of PDTI. Taking part in consensus on qualifying public digital token infrastructure is not a financial service. S1 · s 766A(3)(b), s 9E
ASIC says

Draft: the low-value thresholds are expected to rise with inflation under regulations. Further exemptions for sub-custodians, overseas arrangements, overseas related companies and nominee companies are expected in regulations that are not yet made. A platform that relies on an exemption is still a DAP or TCP. S6 · Table 3, RG 000.76 S5 · para 38

Usually outside the regime

ASIC says
  • Holding tokens does not on its own make something a DAP. The operator must hold them for or on behalf of a client. S6 · RG 000.37
  • Draft: a technology provider that does not possess tokens for clients is not a DAP. Some multi-party computation (MPC) providers are expected not to count as possessing tokens under the coming regulations. S6 · RG 000.41(b)
  • Draft: a "native" tokenised fund, where each token is a unit in a unit trust and the blockchain is the main unit register, would generally be a managed investment scheme, not a TCP. S6 · RG 000.35 note
Our analysis

If you hold your own tokens in a wallet you control, no operator holds them for you, so on the definition no DAP is involved. S1 · s 761GC(1)

ASIC's seven questions, simplified

Simplified from ASIC's draft Figure 1. S6 · Figure 1 It is a reading aid, not a test of your situation.

  1. Is the electronic record a digital token? If not, the DAP/TCP regime does not apply (other laws may).
  2. Do you hold an asset and record the right to redeem it with a token? If so, consider the TCP rules.
  3. Do you hold digital tokens for clients and act on their instructions? If so, consider the DAP rules. If neither 2 nor 3 applies, the regime does not apply.
  4. Are any of the underlying assets financial products? If you run a neutral trading venue for them, you may need a market or clearing and settlement licence.
  5. Are you the operator, or the operator's agent? If not, you may still be advising, arranging or using a platform for others.
  6. Does an exemption apply? If so, no AFS licence is needed to operate the platform. Keep records to show the exemption applies.
  7. Otherwise, you need an AFS licence to issue the platform, and you must meet the obligations that come with it.

Why 8 April 2027 and not 9 April?

The Act says

The Act starts on "the day after the end of the period of 12 months beginning on the day this Act receives the Royal Assent". The commencement table published with the Act gives the date as 8 April 2027. S1 · s 2

ASIC says

ASIC's October 2026 consultation material says the laws commence on 8 April 2027. S3 · 26-245MR S5 · p 5 S7 · p 1 Earlier ASIC pages, including the April 2026 roadmap and a May 2026 news item, said 9 April 2027. S2 · Roadmap S10 · 4 May 2026

Our analysis

This explainer uses 8 April 2027 because it matches the Act's commencement table and ASIC's most recent material. The two dates are one day apart. If exact timing matters to you, confirm it with ASIC or your adviser.

What changes for issuers who tokenise through Tokeniser

What Tokeniser says about itself

Company statement
  • Tokeniser is a Redbelly Network project. It runs on Redbelly, which it calls "Australia's public digital token infrastructure". S11 · Home S12 · Technology
  • It says products issued through Tokeniser "operate under an Asset Issuer's existing licensing". S11 · FAQ
  • It says it programs eligibility checks into every asset (identity, KYC, AML/CTF, wholesale and sophisticated investor tests, and target market determinations), and checks each holder and transfer. S11 · FAQ S15 · Knowledge Hub
  • It uses the ERC-3643 and ERC-7575 token standards. S13 · Protocol Finance
  • It says it is built for professional fund administration, not retail self-custody, and provides key management and recovery for institutional investors. S16 · Knowledge Hub
  • It lists its own "guardrails": the offer is still regulated; regulations can move the line on what counts as public infrastructure; a protocol with a controlling foundation or admin key has to show it is decentralised; and tokens that carry financial-product rights are regulated on their substance. S13 · Guardrails

These are Tokeniser's own statements. This explainer has not verified them. None of the sources we reviewed says that Tokeniser or Redbelly is licensed or compliant under the new regime, which has not yet started.

What stays the same

ASIC says
  • ASIC considers tokenised securities to be financial products under existing law. S9 · 25-250MR Firms must keep complying with existing licensing requirements while the reforms are rolled out. S2 · Roadmap
  • Draft: a token created under a TCP over a financial product is generally the same kind of financial product as the underlying asset. S6 · RG 000.46–000.48
  • Draft: a "native" tokenised fund, where the blockchain is the main unit register, would generally be a managed investment scheme, not a TCP. S6 · RG 000.35 note
Our analysis

Take a fund or company that issues its own units or shares on-chain through Tokeniser, where investors hold them in their own wallets. For that issuer, the Act is unlikely to change the core product rules. The offer documents, target market determinations, anti-money laundering checks and existing licence still do the work. The Act adds rules for platforms, and an issuer is affected only if what it does, or what someone does for it, fits a platform definition.

An open question: do admin powers count as "possession"?

The Act says

A person possesses a token if they can transfer it, stop others from transferring it, and show they can do both. It does not matter that someone else can do these things too. S1 · s 761GB(2)–(3), s 86(2)

Technical standard

ERC-3643, which Tokeniser says it uses, requires tokens to have a recovery system and to be able to freeze investor wallets, pause the token and let an agent force transfers. S26 · ERC-3643 S13 · Protocol Finance

Our analysis

An issuer or agent with freeze and forced-transfer powers may be able to do all three things in the possession test. The Act does not say directly whether compliance powers like these mean the issuer (or a platform) "possesses" investors' tokens "for or on behalf of" them, which is the heart of a DAP. ASIC's draft guide gives some MPC technology providers expected relief, but we found no equivalent statement about token admin powers. S6 · RG 000.41(b) Issuers should get advice on this point and watch the final regulations and guidance.

Five questions to take to your lawyer

Our analysis

  1. Apart from the investor, who can move, freeze or recover investor tokens: us, Tokeniser, or an agent? Could that be "possession"?
  2. Is our product a managed investment scheme, a security, or an asset held on a TCP?
  3. Will any retail clients be involved, which would trigger the DAP/TCP Guide?
  4. Where will secondary trading happen, and which regime covers that venue?
  5. Do our current licence authorisations cover what we will do from 8 April 2027, or do we need a variation by 7 October 2027?

What holders can expect from licensed platforms

The Act says
  • Retail clients must get a DAP/TCP Guide before the platform is issued to them. It must cover risks, fees, custodians and how holders' rights differ from holding the asset directly. S1 · ss 1020AN–1020AO
  • Platform rules have effect as a contract between the operator and each client. S1 · s 912BG(2)
  • ASIC's asset-holding standards must require money held under a platform to be held in trust for clients. S1 · s 912BE(2)(b)
ASIC says

ASIC's planned standards cover holding assets on trust, keeping client assets separate, reconciliation and withdrawal rights. Draft: ASIC may publish a list of firms that apply by 7 October 2027. A licensed platform does not make the tokens on it low-risk. S2 · Table 1 S5 · proposal A4, paras 14, 53

The biggest open question: does Redbelly count as public digital token infrastructure?

The Act says

Infrastructure qualifies if the protocols that apply to it meet three tests, and no regulation has been made to exclude them. First, they must be "a set of open source and publicly accessible standards for transmitting, processing and recording electronic records in a non-discretionary manner". Second, they must "permit any person to contribute" to the infrastructure by transmitting, processing and recording such records. Third, they must not rely on any participant whose role is so critical that the records cannot be transmitted, processed or recorded without it. Infrastructure can also qualify if regulations prescribe it. S1 · s 9E(2)(a)–(d), (3)

ASIC says

Draft (proposed INFO 225 guidance): infrastructure where "only select persons became able to conduct consensus activities" is not PDTI. ASIC adds that "this could include restrictions for KYC requirements", but is unlikely to include hardware requirements or minimum staking amounts that apply to everyone. ASIC assesses licensing requirements at a point in time, not by reference to a future state. S5 · Table 9

Company statement
  • "On Redbelly, all network participants must prove they are who they say they are in order to get network access." S23 · Accredited issuers Users claim an access credential (passport and biometric check) before getting write access. S22 · User access
  • Node operators need network access for two accounts and must stake 100,000 RBNT. They are selected based on "previous experience, location and commitment", and submissions are currently closed. S24 · Become an operator
  • Another Redbelly page says AML/CTF and Travel Rule requirements can be applied as needed "without restricting access at the network level". S17 · Asset tokenisation
  • Tokeniser says Redbelly is designed to meet all three tests and that the network has more than 400 validators. Tokeniser also acknowledges that regulations can move the line. S12 · Technology S13 · Guardrails
Our analysis

On the sources reviewed, it is not clear that Redbelly currently meets the "any person can contribute" test as ASIC proposes to read it. Network access and node operation are gated by identity checks and selection, and Redbelly's own pages describe network-level access in different ways. Three things could settle the question: a change to Redbelly's participation rules, a regulation that prescribes the network under s 9E(3), or final ASIC guidance that differs from the draft.

If Redbelly is not PDTI, that does not mean anyone is doing anything wrong. It means the carve-outs Tokeniser points to for settlement, secondary liquidity and collateral would not apply automatically. Each of those activities would then need to be assessed under the ordinary rules. S12 · Technology This is a question for Redbelly, Tokeniser and their advisers, and a useful topic for a submission on CP 392 before 27 November 2026.

Short paragraphs

Australia's Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 8 April 2026 and starts on 8 April 2027. It makes two kinds of platform into regulated financial products. In a digital asset platform, an operator holds tokens for clients. In a tokenised custody platform, an operator holds a real asset and issues one redeemable token for each asset. Operators will generally need an Australian financial services licence from ASIC. Platforms already operating must apply by 7 October 2027 and can keep operating while ASIC decides. Some low-value digital asset platforms, and people who only introduce a platform as a minor part of their business, do not need a licence. Public digital token infrastructure is not itself a financial product, and validating it is not a financial service.

For issuers who tokenise through Tokeniser, the Act regulates platforms rather than the act of tokenising. Tokenised fund units and shares stay under the existing rules. ASIC's draft guidance says a natively tokenised fund is generally a managed investment scheme rather than a tokenised custody platform. The new rules matter where someone holds tokens or keys for investors, or where an issuer creates tokens backed by assets held in custody.

Redbelly's identity and eligibility tools could help licensees meet parts of the new rules. But the legal duties sit with licensees, and no source we reviewed says Redbelly or Tokeniser is compliant. The biggest open question is whether Redbelly counts as "public digital token infrastructure", given ASIC's draft view on identity checks for validators. ASIC is consulting until 27 November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.
